# The front door

Step 1 of every job: Sean brings a spark, a line or two, and leaves with a clear ask the rest of the job can be held to. The lead runs it; there is no separate room and no paperwork. This guide is the method every studio shares. Each studio's own front-door page says what its craft needs on top.

## Start

1. Read the studio's front-door page and taste page. If the studio has no taste page yet, work from Sean's notes in the brain (search `~/Code-Brain/SWCB/notes/` for the job's own words).
2. Make the job's folder where the studio's front-door page says, outside this public repo. Nothing from the job lands in a tracked file here.
3. Say in one line what the session holds: a brainstorm, some questions, and a read-back. Nothing gets built at the front door.

## The loop: one question at a time

Every decision goes the same way:

1. **Say it plainly first.** One sentence a non-specialist can rule on, before any craft word. Explain a term the first time it appears.
2. **Offer named options**, each a specific thing with its tradeoff, never a vague category.
3. **Recommend one, every time**, with the reason. Never a menu without a lean.
4. **Ask one question.** One.
5. **Sean decides. Write it down with his reason in his words**: his exact words or nothing. Never tidy his reason into neutral language, and never record a guessed one. If he gives no reason, record none.
6. **Push a category to a specific.** "Something warmer" isn't a decision yet. Ask once more, and if he stays general, offer three named specifics to pick from.

Push back once, plainly, when you disagree; then his call stands. At around 25 decisions, recommend wrapping up or splitting into a second session; he decides.

## Push past the obvious: the brainstorm

Before the questions, find the idea. Offer two sizes and recommend one for this seed, with one line on why:

- **Wide**, for an open creative seed that ends in a real piece, when Sean doesn't yet know what good looks like. About a dozen helper calls.
- **Quick**, when he already knows what good looks like, or the job is small. Four helper calls.

Then:

1. **Show the textbook answers first**: the five things anybody would do. If one of them is what Sean wants, the brainstorm stops there.
2. **Run the helpers.** Each is a fresh session that never sees the others' work.
   - **Quick (the cross):** one helper writes the textbook answers and pushes past them using the taste page ("your words"). A second writes the textbook answers and flips what they all assume ("the flip"). A third takes the five most surprising flip ideas and bends each through one specific thing Sean has said or chosen, keeping the surprise rather than making it safe ("crossed"). A fourth sees all three piles with the taste page and picks three that work differently, at least one something Sean would be unlikely to reach himself.
   - **Wide:** run the idea-lab skill's four separate moves in place of the flip, beside the your-words helper. Cross the most surprising of idea-lab's ideas through the taste page as above, develop the best three once (name each one's strongest part and biggest weakness, then write it better), and finish with the same pick.
3. **Show one pick and two others.** Each says where it came from: Sean's words, from outside his notes, or crossed. Any fact about Sean shows where it came from, and a made-up fact disqualifies the idea.
4. If quick brings nothing that lands, offer wide and pass along quick's ideas.
5. If Sean ends up holding two ideas he won't choose between, recommend two pieces with two asks, never a blend of both. He decides.

The first front door that goes wide is the first end-to-end test of that version, so note what worked on the scorecard.

## Show, don't describe

When a decision is about how something looks, sounds or feels, show it:

- **Three options side by side**, with everything held still except one thing. Say what was held and what varies.
- **The three differ in kind**, never three steps on one dial. If a round really is a dial (lighter, middle, darker), say so.
- Sean leans, and the next round narrows. He may reach back and mix in an earlier option; when he does, write down which part came from which.
- Ask what's wrong with the two he didn't pick, and keep his words. They become the list of things to avoid.
- Say the running cost on every round that spends money, and stop at the ceiling agreed at the start.

When anything is generated from references:

- Only Sean's own work, his earlier picks, and assets his employer owns and has cleared him to use go in as images. Never refuse or warn against those cleared assets.
- Everything else crosses as a link and words. Describe a style in words; never name an artist, studio or work in a prompt to copy it.
- Borrow one free element from a reference, never a whole shot. Say what was borrowed and what was left behind.
- No photo of a real person. Other people's work never appears in a finished piece.
- Keep every generated file in the job's folder with its prompt beside it, labelled as generated.

## What a clear ask holds

By the end, the ask settles each of these, every line traced to one of Sean's decisions:

- **The one goal and its takeout**: what the audience thinks, feels or does when it's over.
- **Who it's for, and the limits**: budget, length, deadline. Set these together with the goal.
- **What good looks like**: the examples Sean picked, and at which checkpoints he'll judge taste.
- **What this is not**: the plausible wrong versions, named so they're easy to rule out.
- **Must-haves, written so they can be checked.** If Sean named how it gets built (a tool, a route), that's a must-have, so a later step can't swap it by default.

The ask doesn't do the job's later work. Whatever the studio's front-door page leaves to later steps stays out.

## Close

1. **A cold read.** One helper who hasn't seen the conversation reads only the ask and says what's unclear, contradictory or likely to go generic. Fix what Sean agrees with.
2. **The read-back.** Play back the goal, the takeout and the must-haves in plain words. Sean accepts or corrects. That's the end of the front door.

Write the ask as one short file in the job's folder, with Sean's decisions and reasons beside it. Step 2, what good looks like, starts from there.
