# Designcraft's front door

What this studio's front door adds to the shared front-door guide in craftwork's guides folder. The shared guide sets the method; this page says only what a design needs on top: app and product screens, web pages, and presentations as a side branch.

**Where the job lives.** In a folder named `<job>-<date>`, in one of two places named in `designcraft/folders.local`, which git ignores. If that file is missing, ask Sean for the paths; if a folder it names isn't there yet, make it.

- **His own work** goes in his Designcraft jobs folder beside the studios repo.
- **A design for his day job** goes in the Designcraft folder of his private work repo. Nothing from it lands in this repo, in a personal job's folder, or anywhere public.

Ask which it is before anything else, because it decides where every file goes.

**Screens, a web page, or a presentation.** Ask next which of the three this is, because each starts in a different place:

- **Screens:** the briefs on how a screen behaves and on every screen size lead. The look is tried on the Claude Design canvas or in code.
- **A web page:** the briefs on layout and on motion in an interface lead. It's designed in code, where scrolling can be judged.
- **A presentation:** the presentations brief leads. The story comes first; then a spectacle built in code, or Claude Slides for a clear deck made fast.

Then whether it starts from nothing or from something that exists; when something exists, ask whether it's kept, bent or started again, and open it before asking anything it could answer.

**From another studio.** When Sean brings a brief from Productcraft, read it as a head start: its answers become your recommendations below, and what he locked there stays locked. What's worth building, and what goes in it, was settled there; don't reopen it.

**The brainstorm.** Offer wide when the look is open and the job ends in a showpiece: his own site, a deck that should be a spectacle. Offer quick when Sean arrives with a link, or the job is a screen inside something that already has a look. The textbook answers are the layouts and looks anybody would reach for first; if one of them is it, stop there.

## What the ask must pin down

In this order, one question each, until every answer is a named specific:

1. **What it's for, and what a visitor does.** Who arrives, what they came to do, and the one thing they've done or understood when they leave, written so it could be checked: "a founder has opened one piece of work inside a minute", not "a strong first impression". For a deck: the room, and what it decides when the last slide is up.
2. **Where it's seen.** A phone in one hand, a wide desk screen, or a projector in a room, and which comes first. How people get there: a link in a message, a search, an app they open every day. Something opened a hundred times a day is a different design from something seen once.
3. **The real content.** The actual words, pictures and numbers it has to hold, and the hardest cases: the longest name, the empty list, the error. Say what exists and what still has to be made. Every word is Sean's, or marked as a stand-in. For a deck, whether the story is already written.
4. **The look, one of three ways.** Sean chooses how it arrives:
   - **A link in mind.** For each one: the one thing borrowed (the layout, the type, the colour, a reveal, how it behaves) and what is left behind. A link without a "what" isn't an answer yet.
   - **"Go and look."** Search the award sites and Mobbin there and then, and bring back a few that differ in kind for him to pick from.
   - **No look named.** Show two or three contrasting examples from the shelf, and he chooses. If he says "you choose": Family for screens, Lando Norris's site for web pages, Shopify's Renaissance edition for presentations.

   Then how much of a showpiece it is, from a calm working screen to a full spectacle, and where scrolling tells the story.
5. **What's fixed, and the limits.** A brand, colours, type or code that already exists, and whether each is kept, bent or thrown out. What it will be built in, if Sean knows, and any tool or route he named, which becomes a must-have. The deadline, and any money: a paid typeface, picture credits. Anything that plays the same way every time (a film clip, a rendered loop, a model built in Blender) is named here, so Sean can take it to Motioncraft.
6. **Must-haves and what this is not.** Must-haves written so a screen could be checked against them. Then the nearest wrong versions: the template everybody uses, the tidy one with nothing to look at, the spectacle that buries what people came for, any look he already turned down.

## What good looks like here

- **The shelf first:** `designcraft/research/`. There is no favourite: Sean ruled that the shelf is a range, not a house look, so each job picks its own.
- **Then where great design is shown and judged:** Awwwards and Mobbin through their connections (`designcraft/tools/awwwards-and-mobbin.md`), the FWA, the Webby and Apple Design Award winners, and the makers behind the shelf's pieces. Vet each one for how it turned out, not for how it photographs.
- **Measure, don't admire:** what the eye lands on first and the order after it; how many type sizes and colours, and what each is for; the columns and spacing at phone and desk width; what each screen of scrolling does; what moves, and what sets it off; every state a screen has; and how it re-composes on a phone. Say how each number was read, and what the measuring tools couldn't see.

## What the front door leaves to the job

- **No layout.** No wireframes, and no screen list beyond naming the screens or pages. What goes on each and in what order is the first lock in step 3.
- **No look system.** No palette, typefaces or file of design values. The front door finds the direction with Sean's eye; the look is designed and locked in step 3.
- **No words past Sean's own.** What he gave is recorded; the rest is written in the job, and a deck's story is locked before a slide is drawn.
- **No canvas-or-code choice,** unless Sean named one.
- **No build and no films.** Designcraft decides how it looks and behaves. Devcraft builds it and Motioncraft makes the moving assets, each through Sean, at its own front door.
