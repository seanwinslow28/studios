# Things 3 by Cultured Code (2017, still current)

## What it is

Things 3 is a to-do app for one person. It runs on Mac, iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and Vision Pro. A company called Cultured Code makes it. It came out on May 18, 2017, as an all-new version of an older app. It is still sold under the same name today.

It is a calm tool like Linear, but the opposite kind of calm. Linear packs a lot onto the screen. Things leaves most of the screen white.

See it: https://culturedcode.com/things/features/

**Best moment: the round plus button, which the maker calls Magic Plus.** Tap it and you get a new to-do. Or drag it up the list and let go exactly where the new to-do should sit. One control does the work that other apps spread over a button, a menu and a later drag.

## How it turned out

- **Apple Design Award, 2017.** Cultured Code announced the win on June 18, 2017. It was the app's second, after one in 2009. 9to5Mac's list of that year's winners confirms it.
- **Reviewed well at launch, with caveats.** MacStories reviewer Ryan Christoffel wrote that "Things is full of little delights" and that it felt built for touch. He also called it polished, not revolutionary, and had not decided to switch to it. He listed gaps, such as no way to attach files on iPhone and slow entry of many tasks at once.
- **People pay for it and rate it highly.** It costs $9.99 on iPhone, $19.99 on iPad and $49.99 on Mac, each paid once. In the US App Store the iPhone app scores 4.8 out of 5 from 28,102 ratings, and the iPad app 4.9 from 11,851.
- **The maker kept it for nine years.** The latest iPhone version, 3.25.2, came out on November 2, 2026. In September 2025, MacRumors reported a visual refresh to match Apple's new glass look. The plus button stayed, and now stretches like liquid as you drag it.
- **Later honours, as the maker lists them:** MacStories' best app update of 2018 and best new feature of 2023. I saw these on Cultured Code's own page and did not check them at MacStories.

## What makes it good, measured

I did not open the app. These come from Cultured Code's features page, its getting-started guide and its list of keyboard shortcuts, from the MacStories review, and from Apple's public App Store data, all read on November 16, 2026. Cultured Code publishes no colour values or animation timings, so there are none here.

- **Five lists come built in:** Inbox, Today, Upcoming, Anytime and Someday. Today has a second part called This Evening, for things to do later in the day.
- **Five levels, and no more:** an area of life, a project, a heading inside a project, a to-do, and a checklist inside a to-do.
- **One button, five places to drop it.** Drag the plus button to a spot in Today, under a heading in a project, onto a day in Upcoming, to the left edge of a project to make a new heading, or onto an Inbox target that appears at the left.
- **A to-do opens in place.** Tap one and it grows into a white sheet for notes while the list fades back. Tags, the checklist, the start date and the deadline stay tucked in a corner until you need them.
- **Mostly white.** The review describes open white space, bold type with icons, and colour used sparingly. Calendar events are the main exception: each shows in its own calendar's colour.
- **Touch has weight.** A long press lifts a to-do so you can drag it. On iPhone a small tap from the phone's motor confirms it.
- **Everything is animated, by the maker's own account.** The features page says the team built its own animation toolkit for this.
- **The keyboard reaches a lot on Mac.** By my count the shortcut list has 108 entries in 13 groups. A new to-do is Command and N. Marking one done is Command and K. Moving one to Today is Command and T.
- **The most common actions take one key or none.** Space alone adds a to-do under the one you have selected. Return opens it. To search, just start typing. To add a to-do from inside any other Mac app, press Control and Space.
- **Quick to learn.** The getting-started guide says it takes about ten minutes to read.

### Why it's here

It shows calm without density, and it shows staying power. The lesson for Designcraft: a small set of fixed places (five lists, five levels) plus one very good control can carry an app for nine years. Set it beside Linear to see two different ways to be quiet.
