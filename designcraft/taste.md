# Designcraft's taste

Sean's taste for interface and visual design, in his own words: the things he caught and wants caught next time. It covers app and product screens, web pages, and the layout and look of the things he builds. The lead reads this page at the start of every job, and the front door's brainstorm bends ideas through it.

How this page works:

- Each rule is one line in Sean's words, with the example he caught and when.
- About 15 rules at most. Only Sean raises that ceiling.
- A rule joins when Sean has given the same note about five times and says yes to the lead's proposal, or when he writes one himself.
- Rules come from taste, never from something that went wrong once. A mistake gets fixed in the work.

## Rules

Written with Sean on November 17, 2026, from the shelf, his words on what a spectacle is, his notes on a product tour for work, and the rules he gave Motioncraft that fit a still screen.

1. **"I was hoping Designcraft can take on any kind of request."** No example on the shelf is the house look: each is "amazing in their own way and I wouldn't want to throw those out the window" (November 17).
2. **"I'm a sucker for scroll effects and reveals. Scrolling is a great way to tell the story."** Adding Raphael Segerman's portfolio to the shelf (November 17).
3. **A spectacle takes a design system and will "flip it on it's head. Get interactive, creative, and explore. Get weird with it."** The practice deck that "very much missed the mark" (November 14).
4. **Make it "a spectacle while also being informed."** The show never buries what people came for. The same deck (November 14).
5. **Pictures carry it, with few words. "No one likes reading that much".** His portfolio note (November 17); "a boring presentation with no visuals and spaced out words" (November 14).
6. **"I don't want to hold anything back".** What's already set up doesn't limit a new design. A product tour for work (October 15); his own site, "regardless of what I have already set up in there" (November 17).
7. **It can't look cheap or fake. Motion and reveals "mean nothing if they reveal a dull image."** A phone "looking bare", with "random blocks of UI and text" (November 3).
8. **Nothing fancy for the sake of it. Every piece serves the story, and simple still has to wow.** "A nice 3D UI object, but serves no purpose for the story" (November 2); "Its very simple and doesn't wow me" (October 24).
9. **Nothing "just pops up as opposed to easing into it."** A card on the product tour that "feels sudden" (October 16).
10. **Like things behave alike.** The tour's cards "have subtle animations", but one "has no motion which feels weird" (October 15).
11. **The phone gets its own design. "Trying to satisfy both screen dimensions makes it feel off."** The tour's opening (October 15); a card "smooshed together compared to the desktop version" (October 16).
12. **Everything gets its own clean space, with nothing on top of something else by accident.** A profile photo that "appears over the 'Let's Go' button" (October 16).
13. **The words tell people what they can do, not what the product has.** A script that "doesn't really tell the user what they're meant to do" (November 4).
