## 1. Screens that look good

**The pick: Refactoring UI** (Adam Wathan and Steve Schoger; self-published, 2018; 218 pages).

- No other book here works at this level: what to do, screen by screen, to make an interface look designed. It covers what to make stand out, layout and spacing, text, colour, depth, images and finishing touches, in 50 short chapters.
- Its own page says over 30,000 copies have sold. Goodreads shows about 4.6 out of 5 from about 2,200 ratings.
- **Its weak spot:** it dates from 2018 and has not been revised. It is a set of tactics for web app screens, with little on phone apps, and making things usable for everyone gets only passing attention. It is 218 pages for $99.

**Ebook:** $99 plus tax, direct from refactoringui.com, as a PDF. The page says everything is free of copy locks. A $149 package adds sample parts, palettes, fonts and icons; the book alone is enough.

**Runner-up: Practical UI** (Adham Dannaway; self-published). $59 plus tax from practical-ui.com, a 373-page PDF with free updates. It is newer, cheaper and more rule-by-rule, and it adds chapters on wording, forms and buttons. Its standing is thinner: no rating with a real count was found. The page does not say whether the PDF has a copy lock. This was a close call.

**Free first:** "7 Rules for Creating Gorgeous UI" by Erik Kennedy (https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-1.html), and Anthony Hobday's 28 "Visual design rules you can safely follow every time" (https://anthonyhobday.com/sideprojects/saferules/).

## 2. Screens that are easy to use

**The pick: Don't Make Me Think**, fourth edition (Steve Krug; New Riders, due November 30, 2026; 224 pages).

- It teaches how people really use a page: they glance, guess and click. From that come its rules for clear pages, plain navigation and cheap do-it-yourself testing with a few real people.
- The third edition has 4.24 out of 5 from 31,201 Goodreads ratings, and 4.6 from 102 reviews on Google Play.
- The new edition keeps the core and adds three chapters: on AI, on speaking up for the user, and on using numbers alongside watching people.
- **Its weak spot:** nobody has reviewed the new edition yet, so the case rests on the earlier ones. It is short, and it is about web pages more than apps.

**Ebook:** $31.99 to order ahead at peachpit.com or informit.com, the publisher's own shops (list price $39.99). It comes as EPUB and PDF, stamped with the buyer's name, with no password. Google Play has it for $31.19 but says that copy is protected, so buy from the publisher. The third edition is $34.39 today on the same terms; waiting two weeks gets the newer book for less.

**Runner-up: Designing Interfaces**, third edition (Tidwell, Brewer and Valencia; O'Reilly, 2019; 600 pages). A catalogue of proven screen patterns and when to use each. $50.99 on Google Play, which says it has no copy lock.

**Free first:** Jakob Nielsen's "10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design" (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/).

## 3. How things should behave

**The pick: The Design of Everyday Things**, revised edition (Don Norman; Basic Books, 2013; 384 pages).

- The ideas under every interface: how a thing shows what it can do, how it answers back, the picture people form of how it works, and how to design for mistakes. No screen-design book explains these as well.
- 4.5 from 97 reviews on Google Play. Goodreads shows about 4.15 from about 48,000 ratings.
- **Its weak spot:** it is about doors, stoves and switches more than screens, and it repeats itself. The lead has to carry each idea across to software.

**Ebook:** $1.99 on Google Play today, down from $15.99; the page says the price dropped on November 12, so it may not last. Google Play says the copy is protected. No unlocked copy was found. A protected ebook can still become notes by photographing its pages, as was done for Thinking with Type; it is more work.

**Runner-up: About Face**, fourth edition (Cooper, Reimann, Cronin and Noessel; Wiley, 2014; 720 pages). The full textbook on designing how software behaves. $36 on Google Play, copy-protected.

**Free first:** Laws of UX (https://lawsofux.com/), 30 short rules about how people see and decide, each on its own page.

## 4. Type and grids

**The pick: Thinking with Type**, third edition (Ellen Lupton; Princeton Architectural Press, 2024). Motioncraft already has this edition, so there is nothing to buy.

- It covers both areas. It teaches letters and text, and it has a full part on grids: columns, modular grids and lining text up across a page.
- **What it lacks:** depth on setting type on web pages, such as sizing text across screen widths and choosing and loading fonts for the web.

**The one to add if that gap shows: Web Typography** (Richard Rutter; self-published). The ebook is £14.95 plus tax, sold through Gumroad from book.webtypography.net, in PDF, EPUB and Kindle formats. The page does not say whether it has a copy lock.

**On grids alone:** the classic is Grid Systems in Graphic Design (Josef Müller-Brockmann). No ebook of it was found. Making and Breaking the Grid, third edition (Timothy Samara, 2023), is $40 on Google Play, copy-protected. Neither adds enough to buy now.

**Free first:** Butterick's Practical Typography, second edition (https://practicaltypography.com/). It is a website only, with no file; the author asks readers to pay $19 to $49 or buy his fonts. For layout in code, the free pages of Every Layout (https://every-layout.dev/).

## 5. Colour

**The pick: Interaction of Color**, 50th anniversary edition (Josef Albers; Yale University Press, 2013; 208 pages; first published 1963).

- It teaches the one thing every other colour lesson rests on: a colour never looks the same twice. Its look depends on what sits next to it and how much of it there is. It trains judgement over formulas.
- It has been a standard art-school text for sixty years. Goodreads shows about 3.9 from about 15,400 ratings.
- **Its weak spot:** it is about paper and paint. It says nothing on screens, on contrast rules, or on building a palette. Much of the teaching is in the colour plates, and the paperback holds only some of them.
- **The catch:** no ebook file was found. The paperback is $21.95 from Yale. Yale also sells a complete online edition for $24.99: the full text and all 122 plates on a website that needs an account, with no download mentioned.

**Runner-up, as an ebook: Colour: A Workshop for Artists and Designers**, third edition (David Hornung; Laurence King, 2020; 160 pages). It teaches the words for colour and how to build a scheme, step by step. $18.99 on Google Play, copy-protected. A second listing of the same edition shows $2.99, so check both.

**Free first, and maybe enough:** these cover colour on screens better than any book found.

- Color and Contrast, Nate Baldwin's guide to how the eye sees colour, contrast measures, dark mode and colour scales for interfaces (https://colorandcontrast.com/).
- "Designing accessible color systems", from Stripe's design team (https://stripe.com/blog/accessible-color-systems).
- The colour chapters of Programming Design Systems, a free book by Rune Madsen (https://programmingdesignsystems.com/).
- Refactoring UI's own seven chapters on colour, once it is bought.

## 6. Presentations

**The pick: Presentation Zen**, third edition (Garr Reynolds; New Riders, 2019; 336 pages).

- One book for the whole talk: plan it away from the computer, find the story, then design slides with one idea each, strong pictures and room to breathe.
- Goodreads shows about 4.05 from about 14,700 ratings.
- **Its weak spot:** it argues for calm and simple, which is the base for spectacle but not spectacle itself. It has little on charts, diagrams or movement.

**Ebook:** $30.39 at peachpit.com (list price $37.99), as EPUB and PDF, stamped with the buyer's name, with no password. Google Play has it at the same price but says that copy is protected.

**Runner-up: slide:ology** (Nancy Duarte; O'Reilly, 2008). The deepest book on the slides themselves: diagrams, showing data, arranging a slide. Goodreads shows about 4.07 from about 7,000 ratings. Its ebook price and lock status could not be confirmed. Duarte's site links only to shops for it, so it is not free. This was a close call too.

**Free first:** Resonate, Nancy Duarte's book on the story under a talk, is free to read at https://www.resonatebook.com/, a site Duarte runs. It lists nine chapters and a closing section, and asks for the reader's details first. Duarte also gives away Slidedocs, a short ebook on documents made in slide software (https://www.duarte.com/resources/books/slidedocs/).

**Borrow for the story side:** Into the Woods, on the shape of a story, and Hey Whipple, Squeeze This, on finding one simple idea, both from Scriptcraft's shelf. Animated Storytelling, from Motioncraft, covers planning a story in pictures.
