# Motioncraft's front door

What this studio's front door adds to the shared front-door guide in craftwork's guides folder. The shared guide sets the method; this page says only what motion work needs on top.

**The job's folder** goes in Sean's media folder, named `<project>-<date>` (the date it was made), made with `motioncraft/tools/scripts/new_production.py`. The media folder's path is in `motioncraft/folders.local`, which git ignores. If that file is missing, ask Sean for the path.

## What the ask must pin down

In this order, one question each, until every answer is a named specific:

1. **The one goal and its takeout.** What this piece must do, and what a cold viewer thinks, feels or does when it ends. Three goals means the question isn't done.
2. **Who feels it, where it lives, and the limits.** The audience as a feeling, not a demographic. The place it plays fixes the length, the shape of the frame, and the files it must become (one wide film, or square and tall versions too). The budget in credits or money and the deadline are set here, together with the goal.
3. **The references, each with its "what".** For each one: the one thing borrowed (timing, palette, staging, line, texture, shape, motion) and what is left behind. A reference without a "what" isn't an answer yet. At least one from outside animation.
4. **The subjects.** Each character or thing on screen, with one specific its design hangs on and what must stay recognisable every time it appears.
5. **The hero moment and the signature move.** The one image the piece builds to, and the one move that's its own, with how the move should feel in Sean's words. Where the trade has a word for it (a hold, a smear, slowing into the rest), write it beside his words, never in place of them.
6. **Sound and every word.** Voice, music, or texture and accents only. Every spoken or on-screen word is Sean's, or marked as a stand-in, and tagged locked or draft. Read aloud at an ordinary pace, the words fit inside the length.
7. **Must-haves and what this is not.** Must-haves written so a frame or file could be checked against them, including how it gets built wherever Sean named a tool or route. Then the nearest wrong versions: the wrong medium, the wrong tone, the wrong joke, any look he already turned down.

## What good looks like here

- **The shelf first:** `motioncraft/research/`. Sean loves every piece on it; The Line's Freeview Play film is the current focus.
- **Then where great motion work is shown and judged:** Vimeo Staff Picks, the Annecy and Ottawa festival line-ups, the studios behind the shelf pieces (The Line, Buck, Ordinary Folk, Gobelins graduation films), Motionographer, Art of the Title for opening titles, and Brand New for logos in motion.
- **Measure, don't admire:** length and pace, how long each shot holds, how big the subject is in frame, where the name or product first appears, what each transition carries, and what every asset is there to do. `motioncraft/expertise/film-from-a-reference.md` has the method.

## What the front door leaves to the job

- **No boards, shot list or timings.** At most four key moments in words: the hero and up to three waypoints, each a different kind of challenge (an action, a journey, a face), with camera and framing left open.
- **No look system.** No style frames, colour script, character sheets or palette file. The front door finds the direction with Sean's eye; the look is designed and locked in step 3.
- **No script past Sean's own words.** Lines he gave are recorded; the rest of the words are written and locked in step 3.
