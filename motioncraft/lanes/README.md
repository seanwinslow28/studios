# Lane manifests

One file per lane, four lanes, one seat each, plus [the glossary](glossary.md) they share. A lane manifest is the first thing its seat reads on every invocation, and it is two documents in one file, on Productcraft's schema ([its lanes README](../../productcraft/lanes/README.md)) as Devcraft applied it ([its lanes README](../../devcraft/lanes/README.md)). Written on the Motioncraft build map's **Lane manifests, four lanes** ticket (2026-10-18), from the book-to-seat brief's learning order per seat and the curriculum brief's glossary and principles per seat, both ratified by Sean on 2026-10-17.

The upper half is **shelf labels** for the seat: a title, a pointer into the private corpus, and a one-line note saying when the pointer is worth following. Never the books themselves: the content lives in `../corpus/`, which is gitignored, so on a machine without it the pointers do not resolve and the seat declares `manifest-only` grounding instead of pretending otherwise ([seat preamble](../templates/seat-preamble.md); rule 2 of [`../CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md)). The lower half, from `## Reading path` down, is Sean's: an ordered table of what to read and in what situation, plus a `### Next` shelf of titles the lane holds in reserve. Seats stop at that heading; the reading path is the owner's.

Motioncraft's upper half runs in the order the book-to-seat brief fixed for every seat: **vocabulary, principles, technique, then the seat's own application chapters last**, closed by the seat's audit duty. Each manifest holds, in that order: what the seat must hold **before the first engagement** (the brief's bold items, each pointing at where it is carried); the **glossary rows** the seat owns or shares, by name, in learning order; its **principles** in learning order, each naming its canon title; the **technique guides** by file, the starred six first and the rest named as not yet written; the **application** chapters for the one tool the seat operates; its **audit duty**, with the rows it pins findings in; the **free layer** it draws on, with licence and ingest status; and its **canon**, named, not read.

Four things are different here from Devcraft, and each is a rule.

- **The corpus holds guides, not distillates.** `../corpus/guides/` is the only corpus folder with files in it: six technique guides, principle and tool guides kept separate, each built from free sources and reviewed by Sean. There is no `canon/` of distilled web sources and no book ingest. So every pointer on a shelf goes to a guide, and a free source with no guide behind it appears in the free-layer table with its licence and status and **no pointer**, because a web page is not corpus. A topic no guide covers goes in the artifact header's `thin_lane:`, which is how a lane learns what it is missing.
- **The glossary is one file.** The curriculum brief's 45 terms, and the four naming rulings beside them, live once, in [`glossary.md`](glossary.md), with each row's definition, field terms, sources and the seats that share it. A lane names its rows in learning order and points there; an auditing seat's lane also names, under its audit duty, the rows of the artifact it audits, so a craft fail can be pinned in the glossary's words (rule 6 of `../CLAUDE.md`). Every seat pass carries the glossary after its manifest, and every gate carries it whole, so a seat and a gate name a fault with the same words. Lifted from the lanes on the build map's **Glossary for the gates** ticket (2026-10-19), when a gate was found to carry no glossary and three copied rows had already drifted apart.
- **Canon is named, not read.** No book is ingested: `../corpus/books/` is empty. Each lane names its canon titles with the idea in the manifest's own words, marked "named, not read", and that marking stays until a book-to-skill ingest lands under `../corpus/books/` on its own ticket. Whether a copy is on hand is a fact for the reading path, Sean's half, and never a reason for a seat to cite it.
- **Format, audio and corpus facts are stated once.** A book's format, its audio edition and its corpus state are stated on exactly one lane, its primary; any other lane that needs it carries a pointer row. Listen-only items (the talks and podcasts in Sean's listening order) appear only in a reading path, never as a shelf label, so no seat can name a source it cannot read. Those facts come from the book-to-seat brief as checked on 2026-10-17, not from the copies on hand.

Toolbelts live in the seat file, not here. Gates read no manifest: they work from their anchors, [the gate schedule](../templates/gate-schedule.md), [the gate findings template](../templates/gate-findings.md) and [the glossary](glossary.md).

## Studio reading order

The lanes, in pipeline order (the seat number, as on [the bench](../bench/README.md)):

| # | Lane | Seat | Canon, named not read | Manifest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Story | [Visual Story Director](../bench/visual-story-director.md) | Blazer, *Animated Storytelling*; Block, *The Visual Story*, as fallback | [story.md](story.md) |
| 2 | Appearance | [Art and Pose Designer](../bench/art-pose-designer.md) | Mattesi, *FORCE: Dynamic Life Drawing* | [appearance.md](appearance.md) |
| 3 | Motion | [Animator](../bench/animator.md) | Williams, *The Animator's Survival Kit*; Thomas and Johnston, *The Illusion of Life* | [motion.md](motion.md) |
| 4 | Finishing | [Finishing and Sound Designer](../bench/finishing-sound-designer.md) | Murch, *Suddenly Something Clicked*; Murch, *In the Blink of an Eye*, for the emotion-first cut | [finishing.md](finishing.md) |

Pipeline order lives in the seat number, never the filename, so reordering the bench never renames a manifest. The slugs are the ones each seat file's `lane:` line points at.

In a reading path's Corpus column: `guide` means a technique guide under `../corpus/guides/` carries the item; `named` means a canon title named, not read, with `on hand` or `not bought` beside it; `on hand, not ingested` on its own is a title no seat names as canon; `open` means a free source under an open licence that could be ingested and has not been; `read-only` means a free page with all rights reserved, which a person reads and no seat ever gets as a file; `listen` marks an item that works by ear.
