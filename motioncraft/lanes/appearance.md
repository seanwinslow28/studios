# Lane manifest — Art and Pose Designer

Mandatory first read for the [Art and Pose Designer](../bench/art-pose-designer.md). Entries are title + pointer + one-line when-to-read — shelf labels, never the books: the content lives in the private corpus (`../corpus/`, gitignored), so pointers resolve only on a machine that has it (`manifest-only` grounding otherwise, per the seat preamble). Follow only the pointers relevant to the subjects in hand. Seats stop at `## Reading path`; that section is the owner's.

This shelf is about a look that can actually move: the line of action before the contour, a silhouette that reads solid, volume that holds through every pose, the smear as a designed pose, one world under one light, and texture designed as separate layers with a rule for each. Staging is shared with the Director; timing and spacing are the Animator's; the grade is Finishing's, though the colour design is this seat's. The order below is vocabulary, principles, technique, then the application chapters last, as the book-to-seat brief fixed it.

## Principles, in learning order

Learned after the rows above and before the guides. "Named, not read" means the canon title was not read and no copy is ingested.

1. **The line of action comes before the contour.** Canon: Mattesi, *FORCE: Dynamic Life Drawing* (ingested, ch. 1 and 3 in [`books/force-dynamic-life-drawing/`](../corpus/books/force-dynamic-life-drawing/)): draw the force running through a body, not its outline. No guide yet; the row and the ingest carry it until "Pose, line of action, silhouette" is written.
2. **Every pose reads in silhouette.** Two keys that share a silhouette are one key. Same future guide; Lasseter 1987, p. 39, until then.
3. **Volume holds through every pose.** Designed here, kept by the Animator. Carried by [`contact-weight-and-settling.md`](../corpus/guides/contact-weight-and-settling.md) for the squash keys, until "Squash, stretch, constant volume" is written.
4. **A smear is a designed pose**, as the line-boil research ruled. Carried by [`smears-as-designed-poses.md`](../corpus/guides/smears-as-designed-poses.md).
5. **One world, one light: value first, then temperature.** No guide yet; the row and the greyscale value study carry it until "Value and temperature for one world" is written.
6. **Texture is designed in separate layers, line, pigment and paper, each with its own rule for change.** Carried by [`boil-and-painted-texture-continuity.md`](../corpus/guides/boil-and-painted-texture-continuity.md).

## Audit duty: the moving scene and its evidence pack

This seat audits the Animator's scene against the appearance packet alone, before the motion gate (stake: *is this still the subject Sean approved, in every frame?*). A missing piece of the evidence pack is a technical fail before anything else is judged. Lean on the smear guide and the texture guide above, and look for what the curriculum brief says a reviewer should see in a scene and its evidence:

- the fall's timing chart widens into the contact and bunches at each rebound's peak and into the rest;
- squash lands on the contact frame, volume kept;
- rebounds lose height as approved;
- an arc traced over adjacent frames runs smooth;
- holds state their cadence, and moving holds stay alive;
- adjacent frames around every contact ship with full-speed playback.

Felt weight and snap go to Sean's playback; the audit says so. A craft fail is pinned to frame numbers in these rows of [the glossary](glossary.md): *Timing*, *Spacing*, *Slow in and slow out*, *Weight*, *Anticipation*, *Follow-through and overlap*, *Arc*, *Deformation*, *In-between*, *Hold*, *Moving hold* and *On ones, on twos*, beside *Volume*, *Smear*, *Multiple*, *Pigment variation*, *Squash and stretch*, *Line boil*, *Texture continuity* and *Edge in frame*, which this seat shares.

## Canon

Two titles on this lane are ingested: Mattesi, under `../corpus/books/force-dynamic-life-drawing/`, and Lupton, under `../corpus/books/thinking-with-type/` (both 2026-10-19). Every other canon title stays "named, not read" until its own ingest lands there on its own ticket.

- **Mattesi, *FORCE: Dynamic Life Drawing*, 10th anniversary ed.** — the seat's primary: draw the force running through a body, not its outline; one explicit gesture system that turns into checkable rules. Ingested: [`books/force-dynamic-life-drawing/`](../corpus/books/force-dynamic-life-drawing/). Cheatsheet first; ch. 1 (line of action as Directional FORCE, the rib cage-to-pelvis C and S, leading edge, balance) and ch. 3 (straight against curve, silhouette) are the core, ch. 2 for volume, overlap and foreshortening. Its drawings are described, not stored; its companion videos were not captured.
- **Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, 3rd ed. (2024)** — the seat's canon for type, read for a type engagement, not before: choose and set type by testing it against real content, use the style drawn for the job, and let space, alignment and a grid carry hierarchy. Ingested: [`books/thinking-with-type/`](../corpus/books/thinking-with-type/). Cheatsheet first; ch. 4 (variable fonts and their axes, optical sizes, true italics and small caps, numerals), ch. 6 (kerning, tracking, leading, line length) and ch. 3 (choosing and pairing faces, widths) are the core for a title card or kinetic type; ch. 7 (hierarchy, type scale) and ch. 10–11 (balance, grouping, alignment, grids, serial design) for the frame; ch. 8 before setting any non-Latin script. Its specimens are described, not stored; non-Latin scripts are described, never transcribed.
