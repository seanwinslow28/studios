# BBC Two's "curve" idents (2018 onward)

## What it is

A family of short channel idents (the little films between programmes that say which channel you're on). Each one is a different world: slime with googly eyes, folded paper, fur, pebbles, flowers, glass. Every one of them, at the start and the end, forms the same S-shaped curve down the middle of the screen: the curve of the number 2. The small BBC Two wordmark sits still, low and centred, the whole time. So the logo is the curve, and the curve is never drawn the same way twice.

## Who made it, when, and where to watch

- **Made by:** BBC Creative (the BBC's in-house agency) with the brand agency Superunion (now part of Design Bridge and Partners). Superunion designed the system. The films were made by many animation studios and artists, including Aardman, The Mill, FutureDeluxe, Mainframe, Kijek and Adamski, Ari Weinkle and David McLeod. A later batch of six (2019) added Buck, Weareseventeen and More & More.
- **Sound:** Alex Baranowski. Every ident is built on the same two musical notes, changed to suit each world.
- **When:** First aired 27 September 2018: 16 idents at launch, more added in 2019 and 2021. Still on air after the BBC's 2021 logo change.
- **Watch:** [The agency's case film, 14 idents back to back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TUl3KH4DB9I) (checked, plays). And the warmest single one, [Aardman's "Squish", on director Gavin Strange's channel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ynqIwfrKG5s).
- **Best moment:** "Squish", 0:05.0 to 0:05.6. A blob of green slime with googly eyes is pulled apart, snaps back into the clean pink-and-green curve, and the squelch lands on the snap.

## How it turned out

- **Awards:** two Design Week Awards in 2019 (identity rebrand, and TV, film and video graphics). One of the two Grand Prix at The Drum Design Awards 2019. The entertainment prize at the Brand Impact Awards. The Mill's project page has said the set won more than 20 design awards. ([Design Week winners list](https://www.designweek.co.uk/design-week-awards-2019-the-winners-in-full), [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2019/06/06/carlsberg-and-bbc-take-home-grand-prix-the-drum-design-awards-2019), [Brand Impact Awards](https://www.brandimpactawards.com/registerinterest2020/Entertainment))
- **The channel itself** was named Channel of the Year at the 2020 Edinburgh TV Awards, and BBC Two put that line on its idents. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Two_%22Curve%22_idents))
- **The trade press** praised it for giving animators free rein inside a strict rule. Campaign's headline was about "artistic freedom" for animators. ([Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.co.uk/article/bbc-two-rebrands-idents-give-artistic-freedom-animators/1494104), [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/bbc-two-rebrands-with-set-of-abstract-idents/), [D&AD's pick of on-air branding](https://www.dandad.org/insights/features/BBC2-brand-identity-examples-features-opinions))
- **Why that's believable:** several separate juries rewarded it, and the BBC has kept it on air for seven years and through a full logo change. That's long for channel idents.

## What makes it good, measured

I downloaded the agency's case film and the director's upload of "Squish" (25 frames a second) and measured them. The system rules and the "Squish" shoot come from the makers' own accounts.

- **A fixed 7-second loop.** In the case film, the cut to the next ident comes every 7.00 seconds, exactly, 14 times in a row. The director's own upload of "Squish" plays the same 7-second loop six times, with no visible join. So each ident is a 7-second loop that begins and ends on the curve.
- **The shared shape of every ident (averaged over all 14):** about half a second near-still on the curve. Then a slow build from 1 to 3 seconds. The most movement comes at 3 to 4 seconds. Then a long slowdown from 4.5 to 6.5 seconds, back into the curve. By the last half-second, movement is about a tenth of the peak. Every ident slows into its rest instead of stopping hard.
- **How long the logo holds readable:** in "Squish" the curve sits clean and still for about 2.4 seconds of each 7 (about 1.4 s at the end of one loop plus 1 s at the start of the next). The "BBC TWO" wordmark is readable all 7 seconds and never moves.
- **"Squish", beat by beat:** still curve, 0 to 1.0 s. The slime slams together, 1.0 to 1.6 s (the biggest jolt). A slow, wobbly squish with eyes darting, 1.6 to 4.0 s. Pulled apart, 4.0 to 5.4 s. A clean curve again, 5.6 to 7.0 s. So two sharp moves, each followed by a long ease.
- **How sound lands:** in "Squish" the two loudest moments, at about 1.4 s and 5.4 s, line up with the two snaps. Otherwise the sound stays low and soft.
- **Palette:** a different palette for each ident: hot pink and slime green for "Squish", candy pastels for the pebbles, black and white for the op-art stripes, magenta and acid green for the flowers. The colour changes; the curve stays the same.
- **Type:** the BBC's own wordmark, small and static. No other type.

## How it was made

From the makers' accounts:

- **The rule:** Superunion drew the curve's exact position in the middle of the screen. A detailed schedule gave the exact frame numbers when the two sides of the picture must meet to show the "2". Every studio had to hit those frames. ([Aardman, behind the craft](https://aardman.com/latest-news/2018/december/behind-the-craft-squish-the-bbc2-ident))
- **"Squish" (Aardman, directed by Gavin Strange):** real slime, filmed on a real set. The team tested clay, gels and Play-Doh before choosing one brand of slime. They shot at 1,000 frames a second on a Phantom Flex high-speed camera, then played it about 40 times slower than life. Rather than push the slime by hand, a model maker built a rig that slid the two halves of the base along rails, holding a perfect curve between them. The googly eyes didn't read on the real slime, so animated eyes were shot on green screen and added in post. (Same Aardman source; there's also a [making-of clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_lMqGPFpClE) on Gavin Strange's channel.)
- **Sound:** Baranowski built every ident from the same two notes, so a set of very different films still sounds like one channel. ([Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/24-30-september-2018/bbc-two-rebrands-for-first-time-in-20-years), [Mainframe case study](https://mainframeuk.squarespace.com/bbc2-case-study))
