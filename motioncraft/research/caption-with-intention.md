# Caption with Intention (2025)

## What it is

A new way to do film and TV subtitles for Deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers, where the words move to show how they're said. Each word lights up at the moment it's spoken. A shouted word swells big and bold for a split second, then settles back. A whisper goes small and thin. Each character gets their own colour, so you know who's talking without looking for a name. It's kinetic type doing a real job: carrying tone of voice that plain captions throw away.

## What makes it good, measured

I downloaded the case film (24 frames a second) and stepped through the type demos. The rules and the testing come from the makers' award entries.

- **Each word lights up on time.** Words sit in grey, then turn yellow (or the speaker's colour) as they're said. The highlight moves syllable by syllable, not line by line. So the type follows the rhythm of speech.
- **Loudness changes size.** On "louder", the word jumps to about twice the height of the line, in a heavier weight, for about 2 to 3 frames of an 8-a-second sample (about a quarter-second). Then it shrinks back to normal size over about the next 2 to 3 frames (another quarter to a third of a second). There's no bounce: it just swells and relaxes. "Softer" goes the other way: smaller and lighter.
- **Pitch changes weight.** The rule shown in the film: the higher the pitch, the lighter the letters. The fewer low tones in a voice, the narrower the letters.
- **Colour means a person.** Each character gets a fixed colour from a colour wheel. The design panel in the film shows six characters from one scene, each with their own colour.
- **Line rules stay calm.** Captions stay in the usual place, in one or two short lines, so the system works with existing caption workflows. Only the emphasised words move.
- **Typeface:** Roboto Flex, a free "variable" font. One font file can change weight, width and size smoothly in between, which is what lets a single word swell and relax instead of jumping between styles. The name is visible in the design-system panel at about 0:50 in the case film.
- **Palette:** captions on black or on picture, with a warm yellow default and one bright colour per speaker.

## How it was made

From the makers' award entries and press:

- The team spent ten months building prototypes on famous film scenes and testing them with a panel of 125 Deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers. Their feedback set the timing and refined the typeface. ([The One Show entry](https://www.oneclub.org/awards/theoneshow/-award/64259/caption-with-intention/))
- The rules were written so they could later be automated: sound features (loudness, pitch) map to type settings (size, weight, width).
- Marc Forster helped shape the visual language and took it to the Academy's leadership. ([ReelChicago](https://reelchicago.com/article/fcbs-caption-with-intention-earns-national-recognition-at-2026-addys/))
