# Ordinary Folk's two Webflow launch films (2019 and 2021)

## What it is

Two short launch films made by Ordinary Folk, a small studio in Vancouver, for Webflow. Webflow is a tool for building websites without writing code. Each film opened Webflow's yearly conference and said where the product was heading. Jorge R. Canedo E. was creative director on both. The same writer (Julie Rybarczyk), producer (Stefan Green) and sound designer (Ambrose Yu) worked on both.

- **"Welcome to the Age of No-Code"**, August 2019, 1 minute 30. The opener for Webflow's first conference.
  Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_qCBg_Wsr8M (studio upload) or https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1M4ydNlgP0 (Webflow's upload).
- **"A New Era of No-Code"**, November 2021, 1 minute 44. The sequel. It shows off new features: logins, online shops, and actions that run on their own.
  Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nx_2KIwYEWA

**Best moment:** the 2019 film, **0:28 to 0:36**. The dark world of code tears open into a spinning tunnel of coloured paper just as the voice asks "what if". The turn of the story becomes a picture. Runner-up: the 2021 film at **0:42 to 0:58**, where three questions (logins, payments, connecting other tools) each get their own glassy scene.

## How it turned out

- **Trade press.** Stash, the main trade magazine for motion design, featured the 2019 film in August 2019. It said the studio made "visual poetry" from the script.
- **The client came back.** Webflow hired the same studio two years later. The studio's page says the 2021 film builds on the look of the 2019 one. A client asking for "the same again, but bigger" is a strong vote.
- **Views.** The 2021 film has about 2.3 million views on Webflow's channel. The 2019 film has about 362,000 on Webflow's channel and 125,000 on the studio's. The studio upload has about 4,000 likes, which is high for a studio channel. Brand channels often pay to promote videos, so read views as reach, not love.
- **The studio's standing.** Ordinary Folk later made launch films for Google's Gemini app and for Spline, a 3D design tool. Stash singled out the Spline film because it was built inside the product it sells.
- **What I couldn't find:** no awards and no Vimeo Staff Pick for either film. The praise is solid but not decorated.

## What makes it good, measured

I measured these from the YouTube uploads. I used ffmpeg scene detection (which finds hard cuts), frame grabs, and the captions for timings. Where a fact comes from the studio instead, I say so.

### The 2019 film

- **Length and frame rate:** 89.9 seconds, 24 frames a second.
- **Almost no cuts.** The detector found only 5 hard cuts at normal sensitivity, so about 6 shots. That averages 15 seconds a shot. The first 42 seconds have no cut at all. The camera flies on without stopping, and each shape turns into the next scene.
- **One guide for the eye.** A small glowing ball travels through nearly every scene. It stands in for "you", the person who will build things. You always know where to look.
- **Brightness tells the story.** I measured average brightness on a 0 to 255 scale. It sits between 33 and 88 for the first 50 seconds: dark navy, with streaks of code. From 0:50 it jumps to about 158 and stays there: pale lilac, pink and sky blue. The problem is dark. The promise is light.
- **One idea.** Code locks most people out, and visual tools let everyone build. About 209 words of voiceover in 90 seconds. That's about 2.3 words a second, a calm pace.
- **Structure:**
  - Setup, 0:00 to 0:30: the world runs on code, and only a few people can write it.
  - Turn, 0:30 to 0:50: "what if". The paper tunnel, then design tools: selection boxes, a pie slice, colour swatches.
  - Payoff, 0:50 to 1:22: bright worlds of floating spheres, clouds and planets. The title line lands at about 1:22.
  - End card, 1:24 to 1:30.
- **How UI shows up:** as simple wireframes, like empty boxes with a cross through them (designer shorthand for "an image goes here"). Never a real screenshot.
- **Type:** no words on screen until the closing logo. The voice does all the explaining.
- **Colour:** navy and electric blue, with coral, lilac and mint accents. Measured main colours: #070919, #1d246c, #5056cb, #7589f2, #b9bae0, #e3e8f3.

### The 2021 film

- **Length and frame rate:** 103.6 seconds, 30 frames a second.
- **Faster cutting.** 18 shots, averaging 5.8 seconds. It cuts more than the first film because it has more features to show.
- **Words:** about 214 words, about 2.1 a second.
- **Look:** frosted-glass panels, glossy spheres, white and electric violet. Brightness stays high (127 to 175) with one dark dip at 0:20 to 0:30 (about 65). The dip works as a reset between chapters.
- **Structure:**
  - Setup, 0:00 to 0:42: you proved you could build websites without code.
  - Turn, 0:42: "but progress requires more than websites", then three questions with one shot each (0:48 to 0:56).
  - Payoff, 0:58 to 1:35: data, memberships and logic, each built from glass panels.
  - Title at 1:35, end card 1:38 to 1:44.
- **How UI shows up:** panels that look like the real product (forms, a checkout card, flow charts), but softened and blurred. The text is never readable. You get the feeling of the product, not a manual.
- **One idea, three proof points:** no-code now does logic and data too.

### What Motioncraft can take from it

Software can be shown as a world, not as screenshots. If each scene is designed to become the next one, a continuous camera can replace cuts almost entirely. Brightness can carry the story arc on its own.
