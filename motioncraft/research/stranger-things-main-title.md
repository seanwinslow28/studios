# Stranger Things main title (2016)

## What it is

The opening title of the Netflix series. Glowing red outlines of letters drift past in extreme close-up, so close you can't tell what they spell. Then whole letters slide in from the edges, one or two at a time, and lock together into the Stranger Things logo while a synth tune pulses underneath. It's type and nothing else, for about 45 seconds.

## Who made it, when, and where to watch

- **Made by:** Imaginary Forces (a top title-design studio) for Netflix and the show's creators, the Duffer brothers. Creative director: Michelle Dougherty. Lead animator and compositor: Eric Demeusy. Designers: Arisu Kashiwagi and My Tran.
- **Music:** Kyle Dixon and Michael Stein, of the synth band Survive.
- **When:** July 2016, with season one.
- **Watch:** [Netflix's official upload on the Stranger Things channel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-RcPZdihrp4) (checked, plays). [Imaginary Forces' own upload](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n70OKtp_g6Y) also works.
- **Best moment:** 0:39.5 to 0:40.5. The last "S" slides in from the left, the two bars draw out above and below, and the whole logo clicks into place as the music swells.

## How it turned out

- **Won the 2017 Emmy for Outstanding Main Title Design.** It beat The Crown, Westworld, American Gods and Feud. The music won the Emmy for main title theme too. ([Television Academy list on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Main_Title_Design), [ArtCenter](https://artcenter.edu/connect/dot-magazine/articles/graphic-design-alumna-michelle-dougherty-wins-emmy-main-title-design-netflix-stranger-things.html))
- **Also won** a Clio and a Communication Arts award. ([Imaginary Forces](https://imaginaryforces.com/project/stranger-things))
- **It changed popular taste.** The typeface became a meme and a look that fans and brands copied everywhere. Netflix's official upload has about 28 million views. Art of the Title, the main site for title design, gave it a full feature. ([Art of the Title](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/stranger-things), [Typeroom](https://www.typeroom.eu/stranger-things-the-inspiration-the-alternatives-and-the-final-homage-to-designs-iconic-legendsin-a-single-logo))
- **Why that's believable:** an Emmy is a peer jury award, and the view count and the years of copycats show the public took to it, not just the industry.

This is the one classic on the list (ten years old). It's still the standard example of type that moves slowly and means something.

## What makes it good, measured

I downloaded Netflix's upload (23.98 frames a second) and measured it. Process and credits come from the makers' interviews.

- **Length:** 53 seconds as uploaded. Black for the first 3 seconds, the title runs from about 3 to 45.5 seconds, then about 8 seconds of black with the music still playing.
- **Two halves:**
  1. **Close-ups, 3 to 19.7 s.** Five slow shots of single letter parts: the point of a V, the curve of an R, the bowl of a G. Each drifts gently. The first shot lasts about 5 seconds. After that, soft dissolves come at 8.3, 11.1, 14.0, 16.9 and 19.7 seconds: one every 2.84 seconds (about 68 frames), evenly spaced like a metronome.
  2. **Assembly, 19.7 to 40.5 s.** One unbroken shot, about 21 seconds long. Whole letters glide in from off-screen, a few at a time. Pieces of both words build up from the middle outward ("ANG" and "ING" first), and the first S of STRANGER arrives last, at about 38 to 40 seconds.
- **How type enters:** letter by letter, never word by word. Each letter slides in on a straight line and slows down as it reaches its place, with no bounce or overshoot. Movement is highest at 26 to 31 seconds while most letters are travelling. It then falls steadily to about a third of that peak by 41 seconds.
- **How long the logo holds readable:** locked from about 40.5 seconds and readable until about 45 seconds, so about 4.5 seconds. Even then it never goes fully still. It keeps slowly backing away from camera and dimming, then fades out by 45.5 seconds.
- **How sound lands:** the synth plays a steady repeating pattern at an almost even loudness the whole way through. There's no hit on each letter. The one big change: the music dips at 34 to 36 seconds, then rises about 8 decibels (clearly louder) to peak at 39.5 to 40.5 seconds, exactly as the logo locks.
- **Palette:** one colour. Red, glowing outlines on near-black, with a soft halo and slightly uneven edges.
- **Type:** ITC Benguiat, a 1970s typeface by Ed Benguiat, chosen to feel like 1980s Stephen King paperbacks. The logo's letters were redrawn so they interlock like puzzle pieces, so the lockup is custom.

## How it was made

From the makers' own accounts:

- The Duffers came with Stephen King book covers and the 1980s title work of Richard Greenberg as references. Imaginary Forces pitched three ideas; this one won. ([SYFY WIRE interview](https://www.syfy.com/index%2ephp/syfy-wire/stranger-things-opening-titles-imaginary-forces-interview), [Art of the Title](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/stranger-things))
- Dougherty told Wired they kept it simple on purpose, so the audience could look closely at the details of the letters, like the serifs and the inside of the A.
- To get the slightly imperfect glow of old film titles, the team shone light through physical Kodaliths (high-contrast film with the letters cut out) and filmed the result. They mixed that with digital animation. Dan Perri, who did the original Star Wars titles, visited the studio to share how it used to be done. ([Imaginary Forces](https://imaginaryforces.com/project/stranger-things))
