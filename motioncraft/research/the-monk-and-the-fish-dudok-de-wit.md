# The Monk and the Fish, by Michaël Dudok de Wit (1994)

## What it is

A six-minute ink-and-watercolour short with no dialogue, by Michaël Dudok de Wit at the French studio Folimage, 1994. A monk tries everything to catch a fish in the monastery's water tank. The chase slowly turns into a dance, and in the end the two rise into the sky together. He later made the Oscar-winning *Father and Daughter*.

- **Watch:** Folimage's free full upload on its Folikids channel, <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pUxTfFD4sJ0> (link checked 8 November 2026).
- **Best moment:** **5:20 to 5:56**. The chase crosses an empty plain and goes through a doorway. Then at about 5:38 the monk, holding the fish, floats up into a watercolour sky and shrinks to a dot. The whole film's push and pull lets go in one gesture.

## How it turned out

- Nominated for the Oscar for animated short (67th Academy Awards) and for the BAFTA for animated short (Wikipedia).
- Folimage lists a César (France's national film award) for best short film, the Cartoon d'Or (the European prize for the best animated short of the year), and more than 20 other festival prizes, including at Annecy and Ottawa.
- It is still shown in the touring *Animation Show of Shows* programme (Wikipedia).

Why believe it: the Oscar and BAFTA nominations are on public record, and the festival prizes come from several separate juries. The director went on to a long, well-documented career, which shows this wasn't a one-off.

## What makes it good, measured

**Measured by me from the Folimage upload (360p, the best available) on 8 November 2026:**

- **Length:** about 5 minutes 40 seconds of film (roughly 0:20 to 6:00), then credits.
- **Picture shape:** 4:3, the old TV shape.
- **Shots:** about **35 shots**. Automatic detection found 41 candidates, and I confirmed 34 by eye. A few cuts on dark backgrounds slip past the detector, so 35 to 40 is a fair range. That makes an **average shot of roughly 9 to 10 seconds**, three or four times longer than the other picks on this shelf. Most shots are wide, and the monk is often tiny in the frame.
- **Frame rate and drawings:** this copy runs at 25 frames a second (the European video standard). In the first half, most new drawings are held for 2 frames (on twos), with holds of up to about half a second (11 to 14 frames) on poses. The final chase and the rise into the sky switch mostly to ones (a new drawing every frame), so the ending feels smoother and lighter.
- **How the paint behaves:** it **holds**. Each background is one watercolour painting that stays still for the whole shot: soft blooms of sky blue and sand, hard ink lines for walls and tank edges. Only the characters move, as brush-and-ink outlines filled with flat brown. Nothing boils. This is the opposite of *When the Day Breaks*: all the texture sits in the still layer, and the moving layer stays simple so it reads clearly.
- **Weight and timing:** the monk is drawn as a simple brown robe with a head, but his weight reads strongly. He crouches before every lunge, hangs at the top of a leap, and flops when he misses. The fish, by contrast, has almost no weight: it pops out of the water in quick arcs. The comedy comes from that difference. Movement is timed to the music's beat. Repeated gags (the other monks lined up in a row, about 3:05) land on musical phrases.
- **Palette:** two colour families. Warm ochre and sand for walls and ground, cool sky blue for water and sky. The monk is a dark brown accent and the fish a green-blue one. Night scenes (around 3:35 to 4:20) drop to grey-blue and black ink.
- **Camera:** almost no camera moves. It is locked-off wide shots with strong drawn perspective: the curving rim of the tank, long arcades, crossroads. The drawing does the work a camera move would.
- **Sound:** a score by Serge Besset based on Corelli's *La Follia*, a Baroque piece built from one phrase repeated with growing variation. The film's structure copies that: the same chase repeated, each time a little stranger. The music cuts out as the night sequence ends. I measured several stretches of silence between about 4:15 and 4:22, including one 3-second gap, before it comes back for the finale.

**From the makers and press, not measured:**

- Running time is 6 min 20 s (Wikipedia) or 6 min 30 s (Folimage), counting credits.

**The takeaway for us:** this is the clearest model for a painterly piece that still looks finished. Paint the world richly once, keep the moving character simple and readable, and let timing and music carry it. It is also close to Sean's seed story: one small character, one simple motion, built into something bigger.

## How it was made

- Made the traditional way with brush, Indian ink and watercolour (Wikipedia). Folimage describes the technique as drawings on paper with painted animation.
- Animation by Michaël Dudok de Wit and Guy Delisle (later known for his graphic novels). Dudok de Wit also wrote it, designed it, and painted the backgrounds (Folimage credits).
- The opening card says it was made under Folimage's "Artistes en résidence" programme in Valence, France: a small residency team, not a big studio crew.
- I did not find how long it took.
