# Motioncraft's taste

Sean's taste for every kind of motion work, in his own words: the things he caught and wants caught next time. The lead reads this page at the start of every job, and the front door's brainstorm bends ideas through it.

How this page works:

- Each rule is one line in Sean's words, with the example he caught and when.
- About 15 rules at most. Only Sean raises that ceiling.
- A rule joins when Sean has given the same note about five times and says yes to the lead's proposal, or when he writes one himself.
- Rules come from taste, never from something that went wrong once. A mistake gets fixed in the work.

## Rules

Written with Sean on November 8, 2026, from his notes on the 2026 films.

1. **It can't look cheap or fake. "The transitions and movement and morphing are important, but they mean nothing if they reveal a dull image."** 3D tests "looking like an old PlayStation video game" (October 26 and November 3); a set that looked "like a fake home in a film studio" (October 25).
2. **Don't rush. "Let the audience soak it up for a second or 2", and no cut that feels "as if we skipped a part."** The second work launch film, just after the product's name lands (November 6); "there's no room for it to breathe" (November 1).
3. **A hold still moves: every element "lives and breathes."** He asked for "some sort of movement and animation during that 2.4 seconds" (November 6) and praised the micro-animations on the first work launch film (November 3).
4. **Make it feel real and warm: beautiful texture, a sense of depth.** "That's a beautiful texture" (the painted seed film, October 16); "It looks warm and has a beautiful aesthetic" (October 25); "It gives a sense of depth and makes it more interesting" (October 24).
5. **Nothing pops in or out. Things arrive and leave with motion that says something.** "A phone shell with random blocks of UI and text popping in" (November 3); "the assets disappear… It doesn't feel right" (November 5).
6. **Type gets its own clean space: never peeking in at an edge, never with things falling underneath it.** Words "peeking out the left side of the screen during a zoom" (November 3); a line with "background assets falling underneath it" (November 5).
7. **Nothing 3D or fancy for the sake of it. Every piece serves the story, and simple still has to wow.** "A nice 3D UI object, but serves no purpose for the story" (November 2); "Its very simple and doesn't wow me" (October 24).
8. **When a beat is finished, it resolves clean. No pieces of UI "lingering around."** The first work launch film (November 3); an "everything in one place" ending "with a bunch of UI scattered around" (October 25).
9. **The words tell people what they can do, not what the product has.** A script that was "pretty bland and doesn't really tell the user what they're meant to do" and was "just saying things within the product" (November 4).
10. **Look and feel beat runtime. "I don't care if it goes over a few seconds."** A launch script (October 12). Once the sound is locked, the length is fixed.
