# Productcraft's expertise

One brief per area of the craft: what a master knows that a beginner doesn't. The lead reads a brief when a job reaches that area, or hands it to a helper working there.

A good brief:

- Names the area in a few words and says when to read it.
- Teaches the fundamentals as working knowledge: what good looks like, the common ways it goes wrong, and how a master fixes each one.
- Gives examples, ideally from real work Sean has seen or made.
- Stays craft, not procedure. No steps to follow, no sign-offs, no rules about other rules.
- Fits in about 1,500 words. When it grows past that, split it.

## The briefs.

- `is-it-worth-it.md`: Is this idea worth it. Read at the start of any idea job; it's the studio's focus.
- `talking-to-customers.md`: read before any interview, survey or conversation meant to learn what people need and would pay for.
- `strategy-and-positioning.md`: read when a job asks where to play, how to win, or how to describe a product against its alternatives.
- `reading-the-numbers.md`: Reading numbers and tests honestly. Read whenever a call leans on a metric, a chart or an experiment.
- `pricing-and-growth.md`: read when a job touches what to charge, who to charge, or how a product finds and keeps users.
- `deciding-what-to-build.md`: Deciding what to build and when. Read when choosing between ideas, cutting scope or setting a roadmap.
- `leading-as-a-pm.md`: Leading as a product manager. Read when the job is about how a PM works with people: memos, influence, meetings, saying no, coaching.

Each brief closes with "What a strong product leader would notice", just before its sources: five to seven things a master spots at once. The lead uses that section for the teaching part of every review page.

The books are where the fundamentals come from, not the ceiling. Book notes stay in `productcraft/library/`, which git ignores. A brief in this folder is written in the studio's own words and never copies book text.
