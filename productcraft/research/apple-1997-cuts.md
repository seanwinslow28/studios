# Apple cuts its product line (1997 to 1998)

## What it is

When Steve Jobs came back to Apple in 1997, it was losing about a billion dollars a year and making far too many things. He cut the product line down to almost nothing. Richard Rumelt opens *Good Strategy Bad Strategy* (2011) with it as his first example of real strategy.

**Rumelt's test of a strategy:** a diagnosis (what's really going on), a guiding policy (the overall approach), and coherent actions that all serve that policy. A list of goals isn't a strategy.

Links: a faithful long summary of Rumelt's chapter, https://fs.blog/a-primer-on-strategy/ , and CNN's report on the 1998 results, https://money.cnn.com/1998/10/14/technology/apple/

**Best part to look at:** the before-and-after count: about 15 desktop models, printers and add-ons, against four boxes.

## How it turned out

- **From a $1.05 billion loss to a $309 million profit.** Fiscal 1997 lost about $1.045 billion; fiscal 1998 made $309 million on about $5.9 billion in sales, Apple's first profitable year since 1995.
- **Most of it came before the hit.** Profit came in all four quarters of fiscal 1998, and the iMac only shipped in August 1998. The cuts did the early work.
- **Why it's believable:** Apple's own filed results, reported widely at the time.

## What makes it good, measured

- **Desktop models cut from 15 to 1;** portables cut to 1.
- **All printers and add-on hardware dropped.**
- **Five of six national store chains dropped,** and a web store added to sell direct.
- **Inventory cut by more than 80%.**
- **Every cut served one policy.** Diagnosis: about to run out of cash, making too many things. Policy: a tiny product line, sold through few outlets. Nothing was added that didn't fit.
- **It didn't pretend to know the future.** Asked about longer-term plans in 1998, Jobs told Rumelt he was waiting for "the next big thing".

### What a weaker version would have done

Kept all 15 desktops, promised to "improve quality across the line" and "explore new markets", and spread a cost-cutting target evenly over every team. A list of goals, with nothing actually stopped.

### Why it's here

The clearest right "no" in product history, and the cleanest picture of Rumelt's test. For a day-job decision it shows what strategy is: choosing what not to do.
