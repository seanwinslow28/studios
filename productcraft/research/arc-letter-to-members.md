# The Browser Company's "Letter to Arc members" (2025)

## What it is

Josh Miller, head of The Browser Company, explains why they stopped building new features for their web browser Arc, kept it alive with security fixes only, and moved the team to a new browser, Dia. Published May 26, 2025.

Link: https://browsercompany.substack.com/p/letter-to-arc-members-2025

**Best part to look at:** "Where Arc fell short", with the feature-use numbers.

## How it turned out

- **The new bet paid.** Atlassian agreed to buy The Browser Company for about $610 million in cash in September 2025, mainly for Dia (TechCrunch and others).
- **Arc is being wound down.** News reports in October 2026 say Arc shuts fully on December 22, 2026. Only in news reports so far.
- **Why it's believable:** the letter gives its own usage numbers, and the sale is widely reported. The author had reason to present Arc as niche, so read the numbers as the company's own.

## What makes it good, measured

- **The data behind the call, in shares of daily users.** Only 5.52% regularly used more than one "Space", a core Arc feature; 4.17% used Live Folders; 0.4% used calendar preview. In Dia, 40% chat with their tabs and 37% use personalisation. Checked against the letter on November 12, 2026.
- **The cause in plain words:** Arc was too different, so most people found it took too long to learn for too little reward. He calls it a "novelty tax".
- **A middle option, not just "kill".** Stop new features, keep security fixes, don't sell or open it up yet, and say why (the same code runs Dia).
- **It admits the timing mistake.** He says he would have stopped working on Arc a year earlier, because the data already showed it.
- **A clear shape:** what we got wrong, why we built it, where it fell short, why not fold Dia into Arc, what happens next.

### What a weaker version would have done

Quietly slowed updates with no explanation, or posted a vague "we're focusing on the future" note with no numbers and no admission that the call came late.

### Why it's here

The best public example of a hold-and-scrap decision done honestly: numbers in front, a middle path chosen on purpose, and the cost of waiting named.
