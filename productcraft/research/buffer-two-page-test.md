# Buffer's two-page test (2010)

## What it is

Joel Gascoigne tested his idea for Buffer, a tool to schedule social posts, with plain web pages before writing the product. He wrote it up as "Idea to Paying Customers in 7 Weeks: How We Did It", February 16, 2011.

Link: https://buffer.com/resources/idea-to-paying-customers-in-7-weeks-how-we-did-it/

**Best part to look at:** the bold parts "The smallest test" and "Learning more".

## How it turned out

- **Paying customers within days.** Buffer launched on November 30, 2010, after 7 weeks of evenings and weekends. The first payment came within 3 or 4 days (his two posts differ).
- **500+ users and about 4% paying** two and a half months after launch.
- **Still running** as a privately owned company 15 years on.

## What makes it good, measured

- **Test 1: 2 pages, 0 lines of product code.** Page one described the product; page two asked for an email. An email plus useful feedback counted as real interest.
- **Test 2: one pricing page slotted between them.** People had to pick a plan, one extra click, before leaving an email. That click tested two things at once: do they still want it when they see a price, and which plan.
- **Launched without at least 5 "essentials":** account settings, password reset, editing queued posts, automatic upgrades (done by hand), and the About and Terms pages.
- **Thin spot:** his posts give no signup counts, click rates or test prices.

### What a weaker version would have done

Built the full app first (he guessed one week; it took seven), launched to silence, and never learned whether people would pay. Or run only the email page, which shows interest but says nothing about price.

### Why it's here

The cheapest real test of demand and price on record. It pairs with Cohen's rule: Cohen asks people, Buffer watches what they click.
