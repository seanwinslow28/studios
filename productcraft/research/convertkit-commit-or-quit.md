# ConvertKit's "commit or quit" (2014 to 2015)

## What it is

Nathan Barry's public write-up of how his email tool ConvertKit was shrinking, how he decided whether to shut it down, and what he changed. "Growing ConvertKit to $5,020 in Monthly Recurring Revenue", March 11, 2015. **Monthly recurring revenue** is what subscribers pay each month, added up.

Link: https://nathanbarry.com/?p=3469

**Best part to look at:** the month-by-month revenue list and the section on targeting bloggers.

## How it turned out

- **From sliding to growing.** Monthly revenue peaked at $2,480 in July 2013 and slid to $1,207 by October 2014. After he committed, it went to $5,020 by March 2015.
- **Then much further:** $10,000 (June 2015), $25,000 (October 2015), $97,000 (December 2015), from his 2016 timeline. ConvertKit, now called Kit, is a large company today.
- **Why it's believable:** he published every monthly number, the bad ones too. His two posts give slightly different low points ($1,207 in October, $1,330 in September 2014).

## What makes it good, measured

- **Three options laid out:** shut it down, leave it on autopilot, or go all in. He ruled out shutting down for a stated reason: it had grown his own email list.
- **He paid for the choice:** full time, a hired team, $50,000 of his savings.
- **The real change was narrowing who it's for:** first authors, then professional bloggers with 30,000 to 250,000 subscribers.
- **Sold by hand:** every sales conversation tracked on a board, and each new customer's list moved over for free by the team.

### What a weaker version would have done

Kept tinkering with features for "everyone who sends email", waited for growth, and either drifted or quit with no clear reason.

### Why it's here

A solo maker's hold turned into a pursue, with every number public, and a fix anyone can copy: narrow the customer, then do the hard work by hand.
