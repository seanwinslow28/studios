# "Good Product Manager / Bad Product Manager" (Ben Horowitz, mid-1990s)

## What it is

A short training note Ben Horowitz wrote with David Weiden while running product management at Netscape, the early web browser company, in the mid-1990s. It lists what good and bad product managers do, side by side. Reposted by his firm, Andreessen Horowitz, in June 2012 and included in his book *The Hard Thing About Hard Things* (2014).

Link: https://a16z.com/good-product-manager-bad-product-manager/

**Best part to look at:** the pair about putting positions in writing, and the paragraph on excuses.

## How it turned out

- **Still used thirty years on.** Widely reproduced, and the investment firm Khosla Ventures adapted it into its own working templates for product leaders, which shows it used as a model, not just read.
- **Thin spot:** like all writing on leadership, there's no measure of whether it makes people better. The evidence is reuse.

## What makes it good, measured

- **About 650 words, about 15 good and bad pairs.** Checked November 12, 2026.
- **No headings, no theory.** Every line is a behaviour you could spot in a week, like putting a position in writing rather than only saying it out loud, then blaming leadership when it isn't adopted.
- **The contrast does the teaching.** Each good habit sits next to its failure, so the reader sees both at once.

### What a weaker version would have done

A list of virtues: "strategic, customer-focused, great communicator". You can't check any of those by watching someone work.

### Why it's here

The model for how Productcraft teaches as it works: show the strong move and the weak one side by side, as behaviours, not virtues. The "What a strong product leader would notice" section on every review page should read like this.
