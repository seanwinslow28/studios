# Netflix's proxy metrics under Gibson Biddle (2005 to 2010)

## What it is

How Netflix's product team chose small, fast numbers that predicted the big, slow one: members staying each month. Gibson Biddle, Netflix's head of product from about 2005 to 2010, wrote it up years later. A **proxy metric** is a number a team can move in weeks that reliably predicts the one that matters.

Link: https://askgib.substack.com/p/how-do-you-establish-product-metrics (fuller essays on his Medium page)

**Best part to look at:** the customer service metric that got gamed, and how they fixed it.

## How it turned out

- **Cancellations fell a lot.** Netflix's filings show average monthly cancellations of about 8% in 2001, 6 to 7% in 2002 and 4.8% by the end of 2003; outside estimates put it near 2% today.
- **Honest about cause.** He says correlation came first and proof later: a 2012 A/B test showed personalisation really did keep members.
- **Thin spot:** Biddle remembers 10% in 2002, higher than the filings show. The direction is right; his starting number is loose.

## What makes it good, measured

- **One top number, one proxy per strategy, all in one shape:** "the share of new members who do at least X within Y time".
- **Streaming:** share of new members who watched at least 15 minutes in their first month went from 5% to 20% in a year. Fifteen minutes was chosen as the smallest unit of real value.
- **Personalisation:** share of new members who rated at least 50 movies in their first two months went from about 2% to nearly 30% over five years.
- **Hard to game.** The customer service number was being gamed by hiding the phone number, so they changed it to count only when the number was within two clicks.

### What a weaker version would have done

Told every team "improve retention". No team can move that in a quarter, so every project claims credit and none can be judged.

### Why it's here

Choosing the right number is half of most day-job product decisions. This shows how, with real before-and-after figures and an honest failure.
