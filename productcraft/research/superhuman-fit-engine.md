# Superhuman's product-market-fit engine (2017 to 2018)

## What it is

Rahul Vohra, founder of the email app Superhuman, turned the fuzzy question "do people really need this?" into one number his team measured and raised every quarter. He wrote it up for First Round Review on November 12, 2018.

**Product-market fit** is the point where a product clearly meets a real need for a clear group of people, so it keeps users and spreads.

Link: https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/

**Best part to look at:** the section "Anchoring Around a Metric", then step 1, "Segment to find your supporters".

## How it turned out

- **The score rose from 22% to 58%.** 22% in summer 2017, 33% after narrowing to the right users, 58% after about three quarters of work. Checked against the original on November 12, 2026.
- **Why it's believable:** the founder wrote the method himself, with the questions and the numbers, and the 22% and 58% figures repeat across his later interviews.
- **The business was good, not giant.** A reported $35 million a year in revenue; bought by Grammarly in July 2025 for an undisclosed price, after a 2021 valuation of $825 million. The engine shows whether people love the product and what to build next. It doesn't promise a huge company.

## What makes it good, measured

- **The bar was set before measuring: 40%.** It comes from Sean Ellis, who surveyed nearly 100 startups and found those under 40% usually struggled to grow.
- **One question decides the score:** "How would you feel if you could no longer use Superhuman?" The score is the share who answer "very disappointed".
- **Small and cheap.** 4 questions, sent only to people who'd used the product at least twice in the past two weeks. About 40 answers is enough to see the direction.
- **A clear rule for whose answers count.** Ignore the "not disappointed" group. Among the "somewhat disappointed", keep only those who value the main benefit (speed), and ask what holds them back.
- **Half and half.** Half the roadmap doubled down on what fans loved; half fixed what held back the near-fans (top answer: no phone app).
- **One number to steer by,** tracked weekly, monthly and quarterly.

### What a weaker version would have done

Asked users "what features do you want?", tried to please everyone, and built the most-requested list, with no bar, so "we have fit" would be a feeling, not a number.

### Why it's here

It's the clearest tool for the pursue, hold or scrap call: a pass bar set in advance, a tiny survey, and a rule for which voices to listen to. Productcraft could run it almost as written on any idea that has a few dozen users.
