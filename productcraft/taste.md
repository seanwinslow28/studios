# Productcraft's taste

Sean's taste for product work, in his own words: the things he caught and wants caught next time. The lead reads this page at the start of every job, and the front door's brainstorm bends ideas through it.

How this page works:

- Each rule is one line in Sean's words, with the example he caught and when.
- About 15 rules at most. Only Sean raises that ceiling.
- A rule joins when Sean has given the same note about five times and says yes to the lead's proposal, or when he writes one himself.
- Rules come from taste, never from something that went wrong once. A mistake gets fixed in the work.

## Rules

Written with Sean on November 12, 2026, from his rulings in the earlier Productcraft jobs, the startup's founding session, and his pick from the shelf.

1. **"Real users, real feedback, analytics and results."** A call rests on what people actually do or pay, and ten people who'll pay is the bar for "pursue". His goal for the fitness-app revisit (September 13), and his pick of Jason Cohen's rule from the shelf (November 12).
2. **"Hold nothing back because a decision was already made."** His earlier calls are where a job starts, not walls: disagree with evidence and he rules. Opening the fitness-app revisit (September 13) and the completion-index job (September 29).
3. **"Professional product vision, thinking and ideas, not a grade."** Bring a view and ideas, not an audit of what he has. He turned down a plain audit at the fitness-app revisit (September 13).
4. **"No need to spend and go deep if we can't find anything."** Free and cheap first; money only after a cheap test passes. The startup's founding session (August 29), and paying the $99 fee only if the fitness-app prototype passed its test (September 26).
5. **"I said fire both because I wanted to see if we could expand on any findings to make sure whatever we choose is the right option."** Widen cheaply before a big choice. Two research passes in the founding session (September 1).
6. **"I wanted to see what I could build for the world of tomorrow."** For a startup spark, aim at where things are heading, not only today's problem. He turned down the recommended starting market (September 1).
7. **"We can't keep going back and forth for hours, pointing out small changes, then running the whole thing back. The models won't stop."** A job settles: one round of fixes per note, and whatever's left comes to him. He stopped the fitness-app revisit's review loop at round 10 (September 14).
8. **"The more I worked with it, the more I didn't like the idea. I want to work on something different."** Scrap is a good answer, and his sense that an idea has gone flat counts. He dropped an earlier company idea (August 29).
