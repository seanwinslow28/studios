# Scriptcraft's front door

What this studio's front door adds to the shared front-door guide in craftwork's guides folder. The shared guide sets the method; this page says only what scripts need on top.

**Where the job lives.** In a folder named `<job>-<date>`, in one of two places named in `scriptcraft/folders.local`, which git ignores. If that file is missing, ask Sean for the paths; if a folder it names isn't there yet, make it.

- **His own scripts** go in his Scriptcraft jobs folder beside the studios repo.
- **A script for his day job** goes in the Scriptcraft folder of his private work repo. Nothing from it lands in this repo, in a personal job's folder, or anywhere public.

Ask which it is before anything else, because it decides where every file goes.

**A new draft of an old script.** Sean's old scripts are read, never edited: copy the script into the job's folder and work on the copy. Read the private readout of his scripts first, then ask what he still loves in this one and what he'd fix now.

**The brainstorm.** A loose premise gets widened before it's judged. Offer both sizes: recommend wide when the premise is open and ends in a real piece, and quick when Sean already has the premise and wants the angle, or the job is small: a punch-up, a fifteen-second voiceover. The textbook answers are the five takes anybody would write on this premise; if one is what he wants, stop there.

## What the ask must pin down

In this order, one question each, until every answer is a named specific:

1. **What it's for, and the takeout.** Which kind of script (a short film, an animated short, a sketch, a launch-film voiceover) and where it ends up: a film Sean makes himself, a Motioncraft film, a page for a contest or his portfolio. Then what a cold audience thinks, feels or does when it ends. For a launch film, the one thing the viewer can now do.
2. **How long, where it plays, and who makes it.** Length in seconds or pages, and the deadline. A voiceover runs about 150 words a minute; an animated page plays in about 40 seconds. Who makes it sets the limits: Sean animating alone means few characters, few places and still poses.
3. **Who it's for.** The audience as a feeling, and what they know walking in. For a launch film, the one viewer the voice talks to.
4. **The premise, in two lines.** The direction Sean picked from the brainstorm: where it starts (a memory of his, something that happened to someone else, or pure invention) and the one unusual thing about it. Three premises means the question isn't done.
5. **The tone.** A named example with the one thing borrowed from it and what's left behind: how dark it goes, how dry or loud the delivery, how clean the language. "Funny" isn't a tone yet.
6. **What has to be in it, and what this is not.** Lines Sean already wrote, tagged locked or draft. Characters, names and props that must appear. For a launch film, every fact it may state, with its source. How it gets made wherever he named a tool or route. Then the nearest wrong versions: the wrong medium, the wrong joke, a tone he already turned down.

## What good looks like here

- **The shelf first:** `scriptcraft/research/`, with what Sean said about each example, and the private readouts of his own scripts and finished films in `scriptcraft/library/`.
- **Then where great scripts are shown and judged:** Short of the Week, Vimeo Staff Picks and the National Film Board of Canada for shorts; the Annecy and Ottawa festival line-ups for animation; Cannes Lions, D&AD and the One Show for launch films; the BBC's script library and Script Slug for scripts on the page; and the shows Sean named on the shelf.
- **Measure, don't admire:** seconds to the first laugh, laughs a page, where the premise is clear, what's planted and where it pays off, how long the holds run, where the product's name first lands, words a second, and the last picture.

## What the front door leaves to the job

- **No outline or beats.** The premise in two lines is all the story the ask holds; the beats and the ending are locked in step 3.
- **No jokes, title or dialogue past Sean's own words.** His lines are recorded; the rest is written in the draft.
- **No pictures for a film.** Shots, look and sound belong to Motioncraft, and the finished script reaches Motioncraft only through Sean, at its front door.
