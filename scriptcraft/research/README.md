# Scriptcraft's shelf of good work

Real examples of excellent scripts (short films, animated comedy shorts, launch-film voiceovers and sketches), each vetted for how it turned out: who made it, how it was received, and what makes it good, measured rather than admired. Step 2 of every job starts here, then adds the examples that job needs.

One file per example or small group, holding:

- What it is, who made it, when, and a link.
- How it turned out: how it was received, and why that's believable.
- What makes it good, measured: the numbers, structure and choices behind the effect.
- What Sean said about it, in his words, if he has seen it.

Links and words only. Other people's work never goes in this repo or in a finished piece.

## Sean's own scripts come first

His screenplays from before AI are the biggest sample of his voice there is, so the shelf starts from them: what he did well, and what he'd fix now. They and their readout stay private and never land in this repo. `scriptcraft/folders.local`, which git ignores, says where they are on this Mac.

## Borrowed from another shelf

- **Oktapodi** (Gobelins students, 2007), on Motioncraft's shelf at `motioncraft/research/oktapodi-gobelins.md`: two minutes of pure visual comedy with no words and a cruel last-second snatch. It's something only animation can really do well."

## Shows Sean named as inspirations

His words, November 13, 2026, lightly trimmed. They aren't vetted shelf entries yet; a job that needs one turns it into an entry. He'll add more as he thinks of them.

- **It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia:** "dark humor about characters who don't give a shit about anyone else but themselves. They put themselves into very odd situations and always end up back at the bar like nothing ever happened." And "some of the best cold opens of all time": a quick intro, then a title card that adds to the joke.
- **Community:** "quick wit and punchy. You rewatch and discover new jokes."
- **Curb Your Enthusiasm:** "Larry David is king. His self-absorbed character is how most people (me, at least) feel inside on a daily basis. He just lets it out."
- **Arrested Development:** "some of the best joke writing on TV." Jokes with many layers that you catch on a rewatch, and characters who are "all incredible in their own way."
- **The Office (American):** "dry humor mixed with absurdity mixed with nerd humor mixed with people having to obey their idiot superior." The first American mockumentary sitcom, which led to many others.
- **Bob's Burgers:** "probably my favorite adult animated cartoon," for its dry, witty joke writing "filled with puns."
- **Monty Python, Looney Tunes and Adult Swim:** named as the yardsticks behind his reactions to Too Many Cooks, Oktapodi, Old Spice and Dollar Shave Club.

What these and his shelf picks share: absurdity escalated until it tips over; a cheerful or familiar surface (a kids' show, a sitcom, an ad, a school film) with a dark turn; selfish characters who never learn and reset; jokes dense enough to reward a rewatch; dry delivery over shouting; and visual gags that only animation can do. On November 14, 2026 Sean made four of these taste rules. He left two off the taste page, the cheerful surface with a dark turn and dry delivery over shouting, so they describe the shelf and don't steer a job.
