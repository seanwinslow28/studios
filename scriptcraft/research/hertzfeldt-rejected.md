# Don Hertzfeldt, Rejected (2000)

## What it is

A 9-minute hand-drawn short by Don Hertzfeldt, who wrote, drew, shot and edited it. It plays as a reel of cheerful TV spots that two made-up clients turned down, while the animator falls apart. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UU_m_NYXCek

**Best part to look at:** the last minute and a half, where the paper itself crumples and tears with no words at all.

## How it turned out

- **Nominated for the Oscar for Best Animated Short** at the 73rd Academy Awards. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rejected
- **Widely shared for two decades;** New York magazine called it the cartoon that invented internet culture.
- **Thin spot:** its 27 festival awards are the maker's own count, and its fame came from copies passed around, so the official upload's views say little.

## What makes it good, measured

- **Four steps down, each marked by a title card:** the first client's spots, the second client's, "now drawing with his left hand," then "the cartoons have begun to fall apart." The final collapse lands at 84% of the story.
- **Words run out on purpose:** about 123 spoken words in 8 and a half minutes, and the last 1 minute 40 runs on pictures alone.
- **The button erases the film:** the last figure screams silently as the page swallows him.

### What a weaker version would have done

A loose reel of random weird ads with no breakdown, or a narrator explaining it, or an ending on a gag instead of the film destroying itself.

### Why it's here

A happy corporate surface that rots, step by step, into violence and the void: Sean's Camus short and his mascot stabbing in one piece.

## What Sean said about it

He loves it, November 13, 2026, "for its hand-drawn animation style that feels simple and gets jokes across. That's what I tried to do with Break Time," the animated short he made from his Fetish script.
