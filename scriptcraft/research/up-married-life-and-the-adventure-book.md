# Up: Married Life and the adventure book (2009)

## What it is

Two scenes from Pixar's Up with no dialogue. "Married Life" is the four-minute montage of a couple's whole marriage near the start. The adventure-book scene comes late: the widower opens his wife's scrapbook and finds what she added. The clearest uploads are fans': https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHoHhdbEq1Y and https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IgaRKTBup-g

**Best part to look at:** in the book scene, 0:58 to 1:17. He gives up, starts to close the book, and a photo's edge shows.

## How it turned out

- **98% on Rotten Tomatoes and two Oscars** for the film. Roger Ebert singled out the opening montage (Wikipedia: "Up opening sequence" and the film's awards list).
- **Thin spot:** the book scene is a fixture of fan lists, but no major critic's line on it was found.

## What makes it good, measured

Measured from the two fan uploads on November 15, 2026, from frames. Times are each clip's. Nobody listened, so the music is not measured.

**Married Life**

- **4 minutes 17, 75 shots, no spoken words.** About 28 separate moments.
- **Time passes through things that return.** She straightens his tie 12 times in 16 seconds while their hair greys. The savings jar comes back six times. The hill picnic three times: young, she is first up; old, she falls behind.
- **The death is shown only as who is missing.** A hospital bed, the book handed over, his tie straightened once more. Then he is alone on the church steps. No diagnosis, no coffin, no close-up tears.
- **It ends on a 14-second hold** of the house at dusk as he goes in alone.

**The adventure book**

- **2 minutes 42, 29 shots.** A quarter of it is his face.
- **He never searches.** He turns to the page where her plans stop and expects blanks. His hand rests there for nearly nine seconds. As he closes the book, a photo's edge shows behind the page.
- **Photographs do the turning.** Eleven photos in 55 seconds. Seven of them pay off nothing: they are new, ordinary moments. He first smiles 21 seconds before her note appears.
- **What the note adds is permission.** The pictures already change how he sees their life. Only the note tells him to go and do something.

### What a weaker version would have done

Opened the book on her note. Here he has to give up first, and the note comes after the pictures have done the work.

### Why it's here

The model for an object left by someone who has died changing how the living see them, and for showing years pass by repeating one action.
