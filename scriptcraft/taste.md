# Scriptcraft's taste

Sean's taste in scripts, in his own words: the things he caught and wants caught next time. The lead reads this page at the start of every job, and the front door's brainstorm bends ideas through it.

How this page works:

- Each rule is one line in Sean's words, with the example he caught and when.
- About 15 rules at most. Only Sean raises that ceiling.
- A rule joins when Sean has given the same note about five times and says yes to the lead's proposal, or when he writes one himself.
- Rules come from taste, never from something that went wrong once. A mistake gets fixed in the work.

## Rules

Written with Sean on November 14, 2026, from what he said about his own scripts and the shelf on November 13, his finished films, and his voice samples. Rules 11 and 12 are the lead's reading of his films, which he approved.

1. **"It helps me to connect and write from the heart/experience, but I could also use my imagination."** A script can start from his own memory, from something funny or nice that happened to someone else, or from pure invention. Never turn an idea down because it didn't happen to him. Petey Possum came from "a childhood memory at Chuck-E-Cheese."
2. **A sketch or a scene asks "how that would escalate." A longer arc "should escalate, then settle to a slight resolution."** Petey Possum's "absurdity that escalates with each scene"; for short films, pilots and specs he named Dan Harmon's story circle.
3. **"Fun and also personal."** The theme can be his, but it plays as comedy: Gourmet, "more slapstick/dark comedy and less stress"; the Camus short, "artsy, but also not take myself seriously."
4. **"The brand of comedy that I like" in "a world I was obsessed with."** Cooking competitions (Gourmet); Bourdain's travel shows (Food Traveler).
5. **Visual gags: "something only animation can really do well."** Oktapodi; The Big Snit's "things you can only do in animation"; Rejected, simple drawing that "gets jokes across."
6. **"You rewatch and discover new jokes."** Community, "quick wit and punchy"; Arrested Development, "some of the best joke writing on TV"; the signs and posters behind his own films.
7. **Selfish people who never learn.** The Always Sunny gang "always end up back at the bar like nothing ever happened"; Larry David is "how most people (me, at least) feel inside… He just lets it out."
8. **A launch film can be "an Adult Swim sketch", or tug "at your heart strings."** Old Spice and Dollar Shave Club; Apple's "Crazy Ones".
9. **"Puns belong to a character or a sign, never my own narrating voice."** His comedy note.
10. **Short enough to land, then stop.** "No one wants to read that much" unless it's "a funny story to make the punchline worth it" (July 30).
11. **End on a picture.** A tag after it is fine when it's funny. Break Time tops its last line with a photo after the credits; he cut a written ending for a video (August 10).
12. **Write the holds.** Where his scripts say "Beat", his films hold longer: about twenty silent seconds at the water cooler in Break Time, nine on Petey's grin.
