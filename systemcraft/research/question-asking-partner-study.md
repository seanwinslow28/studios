# A partner that asks questions against a partner that rewrites (2025 to 2026)

## What it is

"Partnering with Generative AI", a study by Sebastian Maier, Manuel Schneider and Stefan Feuerriegel, comparing ways to set up a model as a creative partner. First posted October 27, 2025; latest version March 9, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23324

**Best part to look at:** the results tables that set the four designs beside people working with no AI.

## How it turned out

Two randomised experiments, with 486 people and then 640, both registered in advance. Four ways of setting up the model were compared with people working alone.

- **Plain open chat, no design:** no gain in quality.
- **A partner that only offers suggestions** (three alternatives and tips, no questions): no gain in quality either. That is the evidence that the questions do the work, not the options.
- **A partner that rewrites your idea:** the biggest jump in quality, but everyone's ideas became more alike and felt less like their own (5.03 out of 7).
- **A partner that offers options and then asks questions:** a real gain in quality, and ideas stayed varied. In the second experiment it matched the rewriting partner on quality.
- **Ownership dropped with every AI design.** People working alone scored 6.11 out of 7. The question-asking partner cost the least (5.71).
- **What people did with it,** from a hand-read sample of 20 conversations per design: about nine in ten accepted the rewrite and added nothing, while about six in ten developed their idea a lot with the question-asking partner.
- **The cost:** people found the question-asking partner harder work.
- **Thin spot:** the starting ideas were capped at 10 to 15 words, and everything happened in one sitting, not over several sessions. It's believable for its size, its advance registration, and outside expert judges in the first experiment.

## What makes it good, measured

- **The person goes first and last.** They write the first idea and the final words. The model never writes the final version.
- **Options, then questions.** The partner offers three alternatives borrowed from other fields, the person picks one, and then the partner asks pointed questions about it.
- **A warning sign to watch for.** If the person is accepting the model's rewrites unchanged, the set-up has drifted into the weaker design.

### How it works, in plain steps

1. The person writes their own first idea, briefly.
2. The partner offers three alternatives from other fields.
3. The person picks one.
4. The partner asks pointed questions about it.
5. The person writes the final version themselves.

### What a weaker version would have done

An open chat box, which brought no gain. Or a "make it better" button: good scores, sameness, low ownership, and most people not thinking further.

### Side by side

- **Doshi and Hauser** (Science Advances, July 12, 2024, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532/): 293 writers. Offering five AI story ideas raised novelty by 8.1%, most for the less creative writers, but the stories became more alike, and the writers didn't notice their own gain.
- **Dramatron** (DeepMind, September 29, 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14958): a script tool that works level by level, from one sentence of conflict to titles, characters, outline, places and dialogue, with the writer able to edit or go back at each level. Fifteen professionals tried it, and five scripts were staged at a theatre festival in August 2022. Of the 13 who answered the survey, 84% found it helpful, but only 46% were proud of the result, and more than half didn't feel the work was theirs.
- **Hosanagar and Ahn** (December 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14199): three ways to split a 1,000-word story between a person and a model. Where the person only approved the model's work, quality and satisfaction were lowest and the stories most alike. Where the person did the ideas and outline and the model drafted, the stories were the most varied of the three, though stories written with no AI were more varied still.

Together: person first, options second, questions third, person writes.

### Why it's here

The best evidence there is on how to build a creative partner: the order of a session, and what to watch for when it drifts.

## More details

- Effect sizes against no AI, first experiment: rewriting partner 0.55, question-asking partner 0.36, open chat 0.02. Second experiment: 0.50 and 0.49.
- The "nine in ten" and "six in ten" figures come from 20 conversations per design, so about 18 and 12 people.
- The rewriting partner scored highest on quality in the first experiment. The case for the question-asking design rests on variety and ownership, and on equal quality in the second.
- The study's own section on its limits was not read.
